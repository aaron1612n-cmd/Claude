# Jack Smith and the Great Fence Quest

Every school has a legend. Ours had Jack Smith, Year 10.

You could spot him from across the playground: green blazer, collar at an angle of its own choosing, and a great mass of dark curls that the wind treated as a personal project. He was not an athlete. He had once got out of a cross-country run by explaining, very calmly, that his "stamina stat was allocated elsewhere." What he did have was a gaming brain, a half-smile that suggested he'd already found the cheat code, and a reputation for being impossible to annoy.

---

It was a grey Tuesday in October. Year 10 were lined up along the tall wire fence of the sports court, waiting for a PE teacher who had gone to find "the good footballs" twenty minutes ago and not come back.

Everyone was restless. Dev was kicking a pebble. Priya was loudly regretting her choice of shoes.

Jack was leaning on the fence with his hands tucked into his sleeves, quietly delighted. Every minute the teacher stayed away was a minute he didn't have to be in goal.

"I'm just saying," said Dev, "this is the most boring level I've ever been in."

Jack looked at the court, the fence, the puddles, the one sad cone. "It's not boring," he said. "It's just got no quest."

"No what?"

"Quest." The half-smile appeared. "Give me two minutes."

---

The rules of **Fence Quest** were made up on the spot and written on Dev's hand in biro.

- **Goal:** get from one end of the fence to the other without touching the court. Puddles are lava. The cone is a boss.
- **XP:** earned for style, commentary and dramatic deaths.
- **The Boss:** a seagull that had been sitting on the goalpost for ten minutes and refused to acknowledge any of this.
- **Inventory:** whatever you had in your blazer pockets. Priya's lip balm was classed as "legendary." A single Haribo was a "consumable."

Jack didn't run. He narrated. Standing on the bench in his green blazer, curls blowing, he gave the whole thing the voice of a game announcer. "Dev approaches the lava. He has no health potion. He has chosen violence. Oh, he's slipped. That's minus fifty XP."

Within ten minutes the whole year group was playing. Even the kids who never joined anything were creeping along the fence, whispering about aggro and cooldowns.

The seagull won, obviously. Nobody had a plan for the seagull.

---

When the PE teacher finally returned with a net bag of footballs, he found thirty students cheering a ceremony on a bench. The medals were crisps. Dev was dramatically "respawning" for the fourth time.

"What," said the teacher, "is going on?"

Everyone turned to Jack.

Jack gave a small shrug. "Fence Quest, sir. Level one."

The teacher looked at the seagull, the lava, the cone and the crisps. Then he put the footballs down and sighed.

"Is there a staff category?"

"There's a tutorial," said Jack.

---

They still play Fence Quest every October. There's a leaderboard on the PE office door now, and Jack's name is nowhere on it, because he's always the announcer. But everyone agrees the game exists because of him, and nobody has ever beaten the seagull.

**The End.**
