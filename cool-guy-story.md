# The Legend of the Hand-Rub

Every school has a legend. Ours had Rowan.

You could spot him from across the playground: the green blazer a size too confident, the white collar poking out at whatever angle it fancied that day, and the hair. The *hair*. A great dark cascade of curls that the wind treated as a personal project. On calm days it looked like a shampoo advert. On windy days it looked like a shampoo advert filmed during a thunderstorm. Either way, it looked like it had a plan.

So did Rowan.

---

It was a grey Tuesday in October, the kind of day where the sky is the colour of old porridge and the trees by the car park can't decide whether to be green or give up. Year 11 were lined up along the tall wire fence of the sports court, waiting for a PE teacher who had, by all accounts, gone to find "the good footballs" twenty minutes earlier and not come back.

People were restless. Someone was kicking a pebble. Someone else was explaining a video game to a friend who had clearly stopped listening.

And then Rowan did *the hand-rub*.

If you've never seen the hand-rub, it's hard to explain. He clasps his hands together just under his chin, slowly, deliberately, like a chess grandmaster who has just spotted checkmate eleven moves away. His eyes narrow to a contented squint. A small, knowing smile appears. The curls seem to settle, waiting.

It means only one thing: **Rowan has had an idea.**

---

"Oh no," said Dev, who had known him since primary school. "What is it?"

Rowan said nothing. He just kept rubbing his hands together, slowly, smiling at the fence.

"Is it the vending machine again? Because I'm still not allowed near that vending machine."

"Better," said Rowan.

Here's the thing about Rowan's ideas: they were never mean, and they were never quite what you expected. In Year 8 he'd organised a silent disco in the library using everyone's earphones and a single shared playlist, and the librarian had joined in. In Year 9 he'd convinced the entire form group to wear their ties as headbands for charity and raised £340 for the local dog shelter. In Year 10, during a fire drill, he'd somehow got four hundred students to hum the same note at once, and the deputy head still talked about it in assemblies as "a genuinely moving moment."

So when Rowan rubbed his hands together by the fence, people started drifting over.

---

"Right," he said at last, turning to the crowd. "The footballs aren't coming."

A murmur of agreement.

"Sir isn't coming."

A louder murmur.

"So we have two options. We stand here and get cold. *Or*—" he paused, letting the wind do something dramatic to his hair, "—we hold the first ever Fence Olympics."

Silence. Then Dev, despite himself: "...What's in the Fence Olympics?"

Rowan's smile widened. He had them.

The events were invented on the spot. **The Long Lean**: who could lean against the fence at the most heroic angle without falling over. **The Rattle Relay**: running the length of the court and giving each fence post a single, dignified tap. **Synchronised Shivering**, judged on artistic merit. And the final, most prestigious event: **The Hand-Rub Championship**, in which competitors had to produce the most convincing evil-genius hand-rub while describing an entirely harmless plan.

"I'm going to," said a girl called Priya, rubbing her hands together with terrifying intensity, "*return my library books on time*."

The crowd gasped. Seven points from the judges.

---

By the time the PE teacher finally came back, triumphantly carrying a net bag of footballs, he found forty students cheering wildly around a medal ceremony. The medals were crisps. The podium was a bench. Dev had won silver in the Long Lean and was being unbearable about it.

"What," said the teacher, "is going on?"

Everyone turned to look at Rowan.

Rowan, standing by the fence with the wind in his curls, simply clasped his hands under his chin and smiled.

The teacher stared at him for a long moment. Then, slowly, unable to help himself, he put the footballs down and started rubbing his own hands together.

"Right," he said. "Is there a staff category?"

---

They still hold the Fence Olympics every October. There's a trophy now. It's a laminated photo of Rowan doing the hand-rub, and it lives in the PE office next to the good footballs.

Nobody remembers who won the first match that day. But everybody remembers the legend by the fence.

**The End.**
