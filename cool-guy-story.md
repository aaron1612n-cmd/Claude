# The Unbothered Legend of Jack Smith

Every school has a legend. Ours had Jack Smith.

You could spot him from across the playground: the green blazer, the collar sticking out at whatever angle it liked, and the hair. A great dark mass of curls that the wind treated as a personal project. On calm days it looked effortless. On windy days it looked like it had a plan.

The thing about Jack was that nothing rattled him. Mocks, detentions, a fire drill in the rain: he'd just shrug, give a small half-smile, and carry on. People assumed he knew something the rest of them didn't.

---

It was a grey Tuesday in October. Year 11 were lined up along the tall wire fence of the sports court, waiting for a PE teacher who'd gone to find "the good footballs" twenty minutes earlier and hadn't come back.

People were restless. Someone was kicking a pebble. Someone else was explaining a video game to a friend who had clearly stopped listening.

Jack leaned against the fence, squinting into the wind, and said, "We could just do something."

"Like what?" said Dev, who'd known him since primary school.

"Fence Olympics."

Nobody argued. That was the other thing about Jack: when he said something in that calm way, it somehow sounded like it had already been decided.

The events were made up on the spot:

- **The Long Lean:** lean against the fence at the most heroic angle without falling over.
- **The Rattle Relay:** run the length of the court tapping every fence post.
- **Synchronised Shivering:** judged on artistic merit.
- **The Library Book Confession:** admit your most overdue book, loudly, to a panel of judges. Priya won with a copy of *Of Mice and Men* that was fourteen months late.

Jack didn't compete. He judged, mostly, from the bench, scoring everything with a completely straight face. "Six. The shivering lacked commitment."

---

By the time the PE teacher came back with a net bag of footballs, forty students were cheering a medal ceremony. The medals were crisps. The podium was a bench. Dev had won silver in the Long Lean and was being unbearable about it.

"What," said the teacher, "is going on?"

Everyone looked at Jack.

Jack gave his half-smile and shrugged. "Fence Olympics, sir."

The teacher stared at him for a long moment. Then he put the footballs down. "Is there a staff category?"

---

They still hold the Fence Olympics every October. The trophy is a laminated photo of Jack, mid-shrug, and it lives in the PE office next to the good footballs.

Nobody remembers who won the first one. But everyone remembers who was leaning on the fence.

**The End.**
